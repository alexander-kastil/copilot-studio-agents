# Schedules and triggers

## Content

- A **schedule** repeats a prompt daily, weekly, monthly or at a set time, up to 25 of them
- A **trigger** starts the task when an email, a Teams channel message or a Teams chat message arrives
- The **Automations** page offers Add, templates such as Daily briefing, and the filters Scheduled, Triggers and Paused
- Each automation has an **Enable** switch and a ... menu to edit, run now, pause or delete it
- The **custom skill** decides how the work is done; the schedule or trigger decides when

## Speaker Notes

This topic turns a prompt you would run once into work that repeats. Cowork runs it on a schedule or when something happens, and delivers the result without you.

Schedule: write the prompt as usual, describing the full outcome, then set a recurrence of daily, weekly, monthly or a specific time. Asked in chat, Cowork proposes a Create recurring task? card, and you can have up to 25 scheduled prompts.

Trigger: describe the trigger and the job in chat, and Cowork proposes a Set up trigger? card that you arm with Set up. Teams triggers can be limited to messages that mention you.

Automations page: Add creates an automation directly, and templates such as Daily briefing and Weekly wrap start from a ready-made prompt. The filters All, Scheduled, Triggers and Paused sort the list, and event-driven tasks show Active, Draft or Paused.

Enable switch: each automation shows its recurrence and next run, and its ... menu offers Edit, Run now, Pause or Resume, and Delete. A paused automation keeps its settings and does not run until you resume it.

Custom skill: the skill carries your format and sources, the schedule or trigger carries the cadence. Point a schedule at a custom skill and you get repeatable, house-style output with no manual effort.
